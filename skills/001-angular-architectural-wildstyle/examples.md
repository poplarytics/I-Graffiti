# Examples

These examples demonstrate transfer of the same invariants across scene variables. They are prompt examples, not reference-image substitutes.

## Word-led example

```text
Create “VECTOR” in Angular Architectural Wildstyle as materially flat aerosol paint on one continuous worn-concrete underpass wall. Use connected angular bars, painted wedge joints, faceted painted planes, layered outlines, and painted depth-shadow shapes to create optical depth only. Keep cracks, pores, pits, stains, and relief continuous across every color region. Avoid real thickness, sidewalls, detached pieces, gaps, contact shadows, ambient-occlusion seams, environmental cast shadows, fabricated signage, and physical wall objects. Use charcoal as the field, bone white for outlines, dark teal for painted depth, pale grey highlights, and one rust-orange accent.
```

## Surface-led example

```text
Create the word “AXIS” with a coherent word-level silhouette in Angular Architectural Wildstyle as materially flat aerosol paint on one continuous corrugated-steel surface. Borrow mechanical visual grammar through painted joints, wedges, and faceted shapes; use painted overlaps, layered outlines, and depth-shadow shapes for optical depth only. Keep scratches, seams, dents, and corrugation continuous through all four color regions: deep navy, muted silver, black painted depth-shadow, and one acid-lime accent. Avoid real thickness, overhangs, gaps, contact shadows, ambient-occlusion seams, environmental cast shadows, metal components, fabricated letters, signage, and wall sculpture.
```
