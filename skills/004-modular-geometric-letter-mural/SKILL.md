# Modular Geometric Letter Mural

## Purpose

Create an original, materially flat graffiti mural whose primary composition is a complete crisp long horizontal rectangular painted field, containing one dense word-level silhouette. Its identity is a dark painted scaffold used at selected boundaries, overlaps, and absorbed counters; large pale or middle-value blocks may dominate the field. Build with rounded geometry, heavy overlap without individual letter perimeters, a compact reference-led palette, selective contained tonal transitions, and thin lines that replace specific internal gaps.

## Input handling

- Preserve a supplied word or mark as a coherent collective silhouette. Individual glyphs may be partially concealed and must not be separately outlined.
- With no supplied word, use `Graffiti`.
- Infer ordinary carrier and weather variables without changing the visual DNA.
- Never use a source artist name, source word, signature, tag, mark, or exact composition. Auxiliary ornament is optional, sparse, and freshly varied.

## Construction sequence

1. Paint one wide original word field on a single real continuous carrier in a near-frontal view. First establish a complete crisp long horizontal rectangular painted field with straight, continuous top, bottom, left, and right edges. It is the main composition, not a letter outline or optional background. Then establish the word silhouette inside it; internal blocks may crop/terminate against the perimeter.
2. Build most mass from large, calm, near-regular shapes: rounded blocks, rectangles, circles, semicircles, long contained arcs, long straight segments, wedges, and simple partitions. Use a dark painted scaffold selectively—as perimeter, structural interval, overlap, or absorbed counter—rather than requiring it to fill or dominate every area.
3. Before obscuring the glyphs, establish a continuous word-level rhythm: a clear left entry, a shared sequence of broad stroke beats across the field, and a right exit. Then substantially overlap, crop, and nest those strokes. Every adjacent pair must physically borrow at least one major module or wedge: the prior letter intrudes through the next letter's expected boundary, and the next letter cuts, continues, or reuses it. Deliberately destroy the free-standing outer contour of every letter with a cross-letter overlap, perimeter crop, or shared terminal. Preserve only local recognition cues and the collective rhythm; do not space or trace individual letter outer edges.
4. Select at least three blocks to locally taper, swell, shear, bend, or perspectivally distort. Before drawing any gap line, cover the original counter/stroke gap with the dark scaffold or an adjacent painted module: no large bare-wall hole may remain inside a letter. Do not leave any isolated closed oval, circle, or enclosed counter-shaped fill as a substitute. Instead, crop or open the covered relation into a boundary and replace it with one to three thin contour, echo, or parallel hatch lines that follow the former separation. Use these transformation-linked lines for shallow painted shadow; never add them as free-standing decoration.
5. Extract one compact colour family from the selected reference sample for each output; do not default to blue-gray or reuse one habitual scheme. Preserve a dark painted scaffold plus pale and/or middle-value major blocks as the sample calls for. A contained tonal transition is optional and belongs to only one or a few focal modules; other major blocks may remain near-flat. Its edge may soften, fade, or be swallowed inside hard geometry. Do not turn the work into a fixed named-colour checklist, all-gray fill, flat palette strip, or rainbow haze.
6. Keep the whole mural frontal with no shared perspective grid or global recession. Within selected major blocks, use bold local tapering, swelling, shearing, bending, or exaggerated perspectival planes to distort concrete forms. Paint remains flat on its carrier; no extruded object, physical sidewall, cast shadow, or mechanical assembly.
7. After the word field succeeds, choose at most two sparse micro-vocabularies: for example a small node cluster, a narrow hatch band, tiny rectangular cutouts, or a few fine arcs. They must support a letter relation, never decorate every block.

## Guardrails

Avoid clean display typography, separate letter outlines, large conventional open counters, isolated closed counter-shaped fills, broken rectangular perimeter, sharp mechanical facets, architecture-like joints, ribbon sweeps, loops, woven cross-word flow, shifted duplicate back layers, spatial misregistration, strong perspective construction, default blue-gray, fixed named-color checklists, flat partitions, rainbow distribution, dense decoration, and physical lettering. This style is separate from Style 001 through rounded overlapping blocks rather than mechanical facets; from Style 002 through contained arcs rather than ribbon flow; and from Style 003 through one integrated field rather than a displaced back layer.

## Completion check

Confirm the crisp complete rectangular field, integrated word rhythm, absorbed counters expressed by transformation-linked fine lines, and reference-led compact palette. Reject a run with separate letter outlines, large conventional open counters, a broken field perimeter, default blue-gray, a fixed palette recipe, global perspective, an offset back layer, or physical dimensionality. When a base generation loses required letter-gap lines, use one precision finishing edit on that same image: preserve carrier, field, palette, masses, and letter construction; correct only the prescribed structural lines and counter openings.
