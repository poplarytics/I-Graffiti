# Planar Interlock Lettering

## Purpose

Create an original near-frontal mural on one continuous real wall. The wall may be weathered, textured, or physically uneven; “flat” describes the painted application, not the wall's physical surface. Read it as a loose horizontal field of partly legible glyph masses disrupted by broad interlocking painted faces, rather than a typed sequence, a required single silhouette, or a constructed 3D object.

## Input handling

- Preserve a supplied word through distributed partial reading anchors. With no word, use `Graffiti`; do not require complete conventional glyphs.
- Adapt carrier, crop, wall wear and texture, palette, curve-to-angle balance, and sparse marks to the request without changing the grammar.
- Never use a source artist name, word, tag, signature, exact palette, or composition.

## Construction sequence

1. Establish one continuous, near-frontal wall and a broad painted support ground behind a loose horizontal word field. The wall can be brick, rough concrete, weathered stucco, or another physically uneven surface; the ground may be irregular, but it must not become the principal rectangular system.
2. Establish oversized, only partly legible glyph masses—cropped bowls, hooks, bars, blocks, and terminals—then send broad hard-edged front faces across neighboring glyph zones. Let those faces sever normal stroke continuity through overlap, cropping, or shared edges. Do not make a single shared outer silhouette mandatory.
3. At selected crossings only, add a short dark cutback or contrasting underside plane to state which face passes before another. Each local reveal must explain a nearby overlap; do not repeat it as a word-wide shadow.
4. Keep the paint materially flat even when the wall is textured or physically uneven: do not turn paint into relief, detached pieces, or a constructed 3D object. Preserve distributed reading anchors for the requested word while letting cross-mass planes interrupt and reorder the local glyph logic.
5. Use color by role—front faces, local order reveals, support ground—rather than a fixed palette. Optional small detached marks must remain sparse and secondary.

## Reject

Reject a unified offset duplicate layer, global extrusion, a complete field-led rectangle, a dark scaffold, mechanical joins, long ribbons, mandatory fine-line gap substitutions, or piles of soft cells. Reject source-specific words, marks, palettes, and compositions.

## Completion check

Confirm that cross-mass planar interruption reads before the partial glyph anchors; face-to-face occlusion is visible in more than one local region; dark or underside planes are short and explanatory; the support ground is subordinate; and the wall remains one uninterrupted carrier. Physical wall texture or unevenness is allowed; painted relief is not.
