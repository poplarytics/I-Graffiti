# ChatGPT Image Generation Prompt

## Prompt template

```text
Create an original wide landscape documentary photo of a materially flat aerosol graffiti mural painted directly on {carrier}, viewed near-frontally in {lighting}. The requested word is "{word}". Construct one readable mixed-case word field: deliberately combine uppercase and lowercase forms, retain one or two structural cues per letter, and let neighboring letters share mass, crop each other, and borrow edges. For `HaN`, do not draw an ordinary H with two upright stems and a clear bridge: let its left entry, bridge region, and right-side mass be interrupted, cropped, or swallowed by the same major modules that continue into `a`. Do not draw a closed lowercase-a bowl: make its inner relation an open interruption produced by the H-to-a or a-to-N overlap. The a-to-N joint must use one continuous painted diagonal or curved module visibly owned by both forms; a wedge may cut it, but it may not end at a dark interval and restart as a separate N diagonal. No individual letter receives a complete clean outer contour or clean typeset spacing. Hold the entire painted composition inside a crisp, restrained low horizontal rectangular envelope with straight outer edges.

For a supplied word longer than four characters, do not allocate a separate block or a complete outside edge to every glyph. Begin with only three to five shared broad stroke beats spanning the full word; then let each letter survive through one distributed cue—a cropped vertical, a single diagonal, an open curve, a terminal notch, or a shared wedge. Every adjacent pair must physically share one of those beats, and at least two cues must be interrupted by a neighboring letter. The word must be recoverable as a rhythm after inspection, never legible by reading a row of separate display letters.

For the three-letter negative-transfer word `MNH`, use only four giant modules. One pale diagonal must begin within the M zone, continue through the N zone, and remain physically present at the H side; a second dark/olive wedge must cut across both joins. The M and N must borrow one rounded hump, while N and H must share a terminal/stem mass. These are two continuous cross-letter modules, not aligned adjacent glyph strokes. Do not permit a clean M, then N, then H reading order.

For zero-input `Graffiti`, distribute the recognition cues across the field: no one clear initial letter may occupy more than one quarter of the width or retain a complete outside contour. Force the first broad curve to be cut by the next shared module before it closes, and use rounded wedges rather than a dense sawtooth of sharp triangles.

Build most visual mass from large calm near-regular shapes: rounded blocks, rectangles, circles, semicircles, long contained arcs, long straight segments, and simple partitions. Generous rounded corners are common. Use a small number of large, clean triangular or wedge counter-cuts as abrupt high-contrast incursions into rounded mass; every wedge must reconnect a stroke, a local deformation, or a compressed gap, never form a small mechanical facet pattern. Substantially overlap, crop, and nest broad strokes; do not trace individual letter outer perimeters.

Fully spray-paint the complete rectangular field using the chosen role hierarchy; every paint edge, drip, and curve must stay inside its straight perimeter. A continuous dark role forms border, interval, counter fill, or structural skeleton; it is never exposed carrier but does not need to dominate the field. Add high-contrast pale and mid-value structural panels; many may be near-flat fills. Use {palette_trajectory} only in one or a few focal modules as a narrow related bloom. Choose one compact palette family for this output; no palette is default. CRITICAL: never leave a bare-wall counter, conventional closed circular/oval hole, or isolated closed counter-shaped colored fill inside a letter. First bury every intended H/a/N internal gap inside a broad painted dark scaffold or adjacent painted overlap; then open only a short local relation within that covered interior, away from the outer silhouette and away from the rectangle perimeter. Its fine stroke must sit across or immediately inside the dark covered gap—not trace a pale block's outer edge—so it reads as the remaining evidence of a compressed separation rather than a contour. H has two covered gaps, one above and one below its bridge, expressed by exactly two physical fine strokes total: one short unpaired stroke for the upper gap and one short unpaired stroke for the lower gap. Do not duplicate, echo, or hatch either H stroke. The a uses one short attached curve to mark its absorbed inner relation; it must not contain an isolated enclosed bowl/counter. A strongly transformed N has one covered internal separation, expressed only by one fine line. Optionally use one or two sparse micro-structure vocabularies—white/black circular nodes, contained halftone-dot clusters, narrow vertical hatch bands, or small rectangular cutouts—to index joins; never blanket the mural in ornament. Keep the whole field frontal, with no shared vanishing point or global recession. Do not add free-standing decorative lines.

After the word field succeeds, use only sparse subordinate dots, hatching, fine arcs, short lines, small blocks, or limited drips. Create a new composition without copying a source artist, source text, signature, tag, mark, character, or layout.

Avoid clean display typography, even letter spacing, individual letter outlines, large conventional open counters, sharp mechanical facets, engineered joints, deep extrusion, ribbon sweeps, loops, hooks, woven multi-letter flow, large shifted duplicate back layers, spatial misregistration, offset-shadow systems, perspective grids, dramatic recession, physical signage, relief, sidewalls, detached pieces, floating letters, cast shadows, watermark, added prose, default blue-gray coloration, or flat color-block partitions.
```

## Validation controls

## Priority lock for dense prompts

When generation pressure forces a tradeoff, preserve in this order: (1) the full painted rectangle, (2) the specific letter-gap line count and attachment, (3) shared H-to-a and a-to-N major modules/wedges, (4) rounded-mass construction, then (5) palette variation and optional microstructure. Do not drop required structural gap lines merely to add palette detail, texture, extra blocks, or ornament.

## Precision finishing edit

If a base generation satisfies the field, shared modules, and palette but loses one or more required line substitutions, perform one image-generation edit on that specific base. Preserve the carrier, rectangle, palette roles, large modules, wedges, and word construction. Add, remove, or reposition only the named gap lines and any enclosed counter that prevents their reading. Each line must be placed in the interior of the existing dark covered relation, not along a pale mass perimeter or the outer mural border. Each independent base image receives its own edit; do not use an edited image as the base of another validation run.

For two independent comparison outputs, vary only the requested word or scene controls. Keep the full prompt, generator invocation, aspect ratio, carrier, viewpoint, lighting, palette roles, and accent control identical within the pair.
